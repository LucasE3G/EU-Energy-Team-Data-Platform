What the heat did to Europe’s power system

How a heatwave day is defined

Three or more consecutive days above the local day-of-year 90th percentile of daily maximum temperature (2014–2025 baseline), or at or above 30 °C. Temperature is ERA5 reanalysis, population-weighted across each country’s largest metropolitan areas, so it tracks where demand actually sits.

What the comparison holds fixed

Heatwave days are compared against non-heatwave days from the same May–September season, with weekdays matched to weekdays and weekends to weekends — without that, a heatwave falling on a working week reads as demand growth that is really just the difference between a Tuesday and a Sunday.

Why 19 countries and not 27

    Switzerland is shown alongside the EU members because it sits inside the same synchronous grid and trades across it.

    Reading the country card

    The bars are supply-side: generation by fuel plus net cross-border trade, as a change in average daily energy. They will not sum exactly to the demand change, because a country also exports, pumps and loses energy in transmission.
